Current naming context:
- The cloud-infrastructure AI agent formerly named `Finn` is now named `Paver`.
- The AI benchmarking product was called `Finnsight`, based on the old Finn identity, and needs a replacement name independent of Finn. It does not need to incorporate Paver and should not be forced into infrastructure or construction imagery.
- The benchmark product compares AI models, agents, and broader AI systems. Its identity should be credible for serious developer and enterprise use while remaining cool, concise, memorable, and reasonably ownable.
- Preferred naming styles are concise coined names, compounds, or evocative real words. Because the team expects to say the product name frequently in internal conversation, spoken usability is a primary filter: it should roll off the tongue, feel natural in routine phrases, and resist awkward shortening. Useful semantic territories include measurement, trials, evaluation, intelligence, clarity, arenas, standards, navigation, and performance.
- Avoid generic names that are difficult to own, awkward spellings, obvious copies of established AI benchmark brands, and names derived mechanically from Paver.
- If proposing a Paver-linked successor to `Finnsight`, preserve the complete string `Paver`; clipped constructions such as `Paveval` or `Pavaluation` are unacceptable. Use a genuine portmanteau or word overlap whose second meaning remains recognizable (as `Finn` + `insight` is), not a simple compound such as `PaverSight`. Prior overlap candidates including `Paverdict`, `Paverify`, `Pavertex`, `Paverall`, and `Paverity` have not produced an accepted direction and should not be treated as finalists. Exact `Paver` wordplay appears overconstrained because it lacks a natural overlap with evaluation vocabulary; prioritize a strong standalone benchmark name rather than manufacturing more Paver suffixes unless the constraint changes.
- The name should work both as a standalone product and within the OpenVibely ecosystem.
- This is a product name for an existing enterprise company with its own established company domain, not a request for a new domain name. Domain availability is therefore not a naming criterion; prioritize product-name collisions, ownability, and trademark risk instead.
- No replacement benchmark name has been selected in the captured context. Any future shortlist should include a best-effort public collision check, with explicit acknowledgment that this is not legal trademark clearance.
